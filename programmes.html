From first day to Tulip.

Four groups, one idea: children learn best when they are doing something. Class groupings follow the school's own arrangement — call us and we will tell you which class suits your child.

First steps away from home

Apricot Baby Class

The gentlest start we can give. For many children this is the first long stretch of the day away from home, so settling comes before everything else.

Days are built on a predictable rhythm — greeting, songs, play, food, rest — because a child who knows what happens next is a child free to explore. We work on sharing, taking turns, naming feelings, holding a crayon, and simply being happy in a room full of other children.

  • Songs, rhymes and movement every day
  • Free play with blocks, shapes and colour
  • Toilet routines, washing hands, eating together
  • Gentle introduction to letters, numbers and sounds

Letters, numbers, confidence

Lavender Middle Class

The middle year is where the foundations are laid — the sounds of letters, the shape of numbers, and the habit of asking why.

Children work in small groups so a teacher can sit with them. Reading starts with stories and sounds rather than drills; number work starts with counting real things. Writing grows out of drawing, which grows out of play.

  • Phonics, letter formation and early reading
  • Counting, sorting, patterns and early number work
  • Stories, news time and speaking in front of the class
  • Drawing, cutting, modelling and other fine-motor work

Letters, numbers, confidence

Jasmine Top Class

The top year turns those foundations into school habits, so that Tulip feels like the next step rather than a leap.

The groups stay small and the work gets longer: reading on from sounds to sentences, writing on from letters to lines, and number work on from counting to adding. Children speak in front of the class and begin to manage their own books and belongings.

  • Reading sentences, not only sounds
  • Writing on lines, and early adding
  • Speaking up, and looking after their own things
  • Drawing, cutting, modelling and other fine-motor work

The first primary year

Tulip Primary One

Tulip is a big step, and children take it better from a place that already knows them.

The work becomes more formal — reading, writing and number work with books and exercises — but the care does not change. Small groups mean a teacher notices quickly when a child is stuck, and the day still has room in it for play, music and running about.

  • Reading and comprehension
  • Handwriting and written work
  • Number work, with real objects before symbols
  • Regular feedback to parents on how a child is doing

Through the week

Kids Activity — where hidden talents show up first.

Music & movement

Singing, clapping, drumming and dancing — rhythm that children feel in their whole body before they ever read a note.

Art & making

Painting, cutting, modelling and building. Messy work with real materials, and the patience to let a child finish it.

Outdoor play

Running, ball games and group play in the open air — the fastest way children learn fairness, turn-taking and how to lose well.

A typical day

How our day flows.

An outline of the rhythm children settle into. Exact class times are confirmed when you enrol.

7:00 am

Arrival of learners

Children are received and settled, and greeted by name.

8:00 am

Circle time

Songs, prayer, the day's news and a story together.

8:30 am

Classes

Letters, numbers and hands-on work in small groups.

10:00 am

Break & play time

Fresh air, running and group games.

11:00 am

Classes

Back to small groups for the second stretch of the morning.

12:30 pm

Snack time

A short break with something to eat.

1:00 pm

Lunch time

A proper break, eating together.

2:00 pm

Rest & entertainment

Quiet time to rest, then something to enjoy together.

3:30 pm

Snack time

A second short break before the end of the day.

4:00 pm

Pick-up time

Tidy up, a word with parents, and off home.
